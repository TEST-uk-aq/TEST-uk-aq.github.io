Understanding the signal

How Black Carbon and UV help identify wood-smoke patterns

Black Carbon measured at 880 nm responds to black-carbon-containing emissions from traffic and other combustion sources.

The 370 nm channel is more sensitive to additional UV-absorbing material associated with sources such as biomass and wood smoke.

Comparing the channels, including the UV-minus-BC difference shown here as UVPM, can reveal patterns more consistent with biomass burning. This is useful source evidence, not proof that an individual concentration came from domestic wood burning.

What is Black Carbon?

Black Carbon is a component of particulate pollution produced by incomplete combustion. It is not specific to domestic wood burning; road transport and other fuel combustion may also contribute.

Wood burning and air pollution

Smoke from wood combustion contains particles and can affect indoor and outdoor air. The amount measured at a monitor varies with local conditions, combustion sources and weather.

Monitoring and limitations

Black Carbon monitoring is sparse compared with common pollutants. A monitor describes its own location, not a whole town, authority, region or the UK. Concentration alone does not establish source attribution, and the latest source day may lag the browser date.
